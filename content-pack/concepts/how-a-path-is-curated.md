## What a path is

<!-- framing -->
A path is one curated row: one plant sense, written as a chain of six stages. It runs from the **stimulus** (what is sensed) to the **receptor / transducer** (the molecule that detects it), the **cascade** of signalling steps inside the cell, the **signal route** by which the message travels beyond that cell, the **integration** where it is weighed against other cues, and the **response**, what the plant does.

<!-- framing -->
Each stage was drafted from the abstracts of verified references and accepted in curation. A stage with no verified source is left as a **gap**, drawn as a dashed card: unknown, not absent. Signal routes are the commonest gap.

## How much is known

<!-- framing -->
Each path is graded rather than asserted. The **receptor tier** grades the sensing molecule: B1 established, B2 partial, B3 unknown or inferred. **⚑** marks a field that is contested or only partly supported, or a path that sits in two branches. **Provenance** says whether the text tracks a verified abstract (A) or is curator knowledge (K). The **evidence species** says which organism the key evidence comes from; Arabidopsis thaliana unless the path carries the 🌱 other-species badge.

<!-- framing -->
A reference counts as **verified** when its DOI and title were matched to the bibliographic record and its abstract was read. That is checking at abstract level, not full text.

## Where the app adds links

<!-- framing -->
Some links are the curators' choices rather than facts from a source: a stage value matched to a Plant Ontology term, or a family name expanded to gene records. These are labelled **curator mapping** wherever they appear.

## How to use this in the app

<!-- framing -->
Open any path and read its stages in order. Click a card to open the inspector, which shows the other paths through that stage and the evidence. Where a stage is dashed, nobody has sourced it yet.
